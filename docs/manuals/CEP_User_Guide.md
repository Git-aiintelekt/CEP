# Welcome to C360

C360 is the Customer Engagement Platform: the place where a marketing team keeps its contacts, designs emails and texts, sends campaigns and reads what came back. This guide is for the people who sign in to a workspace: administrators who set it up, marketers who run campaigns, and anyone who reads the reports.

A second guide, the Platform Guide, is for the platform team who run the back-office: creating workspaces, plans, features and the platform's own settings. If you were invited to a workspace, you only need this one.

## How the guide is organised

Every page of the app has a chapter, and every chapter has the same shape:

- **What this page is for.** One paragraph on the job the page does.
- **Who can use it.** The permission a role needs, and the feature the workspace needs switched on.
- **The fields and controls.** A table of everything on the page: what each field or button is, and what it changes elsewhere in C360. That is the part to read when a setting is unclear.
- **How to.** The everyday tasks, step by step.
- **What happens behind the scenes.** The business flow: what C360 does with what you entered, and where it shows up later.
- **Things to know.** The limits and the consequences worth remembering.

## Help inside the app

- **The help icon** beside a page's title opens that page's chapter in a panel, so the guide is one click away from the page it describes. Close it with the cross or the Escape key.
- **The small information icon** beside a field opens a note on that one field: what it is and what it affects.
- **The keyboard icon** in the header lists the keyboard shortcuts. Pressing `?` on any page does the same.
- **The foot of every page** carries the platform's copyright line and a link to the Terms of Use, which also opens from the sign-in page without signing in.

## Three ideas that run through everything

**Workspace.** Your company's own area of C360. Everything you see belongs to one workspace: its users, roles, lists, templates, campaigns and settings. The workspace code (for example `NWA`) appears in the sign-in screen and in every address C360 gives you to register with other systems.

**Roles and permissions.** What a person may do is set by their role. A role is a set of permissions such as *campaign.edit* or *user.admin*. The chapter for each page names the permission it needs. If a page refuses you, it says which permission to ask an administrator for.

**Features.** Some parts of C360 are switched on per workspace by the platform team: texts, MMS, e-receipts, journeys, A/B testing, API access, the preference centre, barcodes. A page that belongs to a feature that is off is simply not shown, and its chapter says which feature to ask for.

## Statuses you will meet

| Status | Where | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Draft | campaigns, lists | Being prepared; nothing has been sent and nothing is live. |
| Test Published | campaigns | Published to test lists only. Production lists cannot be attached. |
| Production Published | campaigns | Published to real people; a scheduled run will go out. |
| Scheduled | campaigns | A production campaign with a run waiting for its time. |
| Active, Sending, Preparing | campaigns | Live right now: a run is building its audience or sending. |
| Completed | campaigns | The run finished. |
| Verified, Not Verified Yet, Verification Failed | senders | Whether a sender address or number has proven it can send. |

## Where to start

A new workspace begins with the chapter **Setting Up a New Workspace**: from the invitation email and the first sign-in through the seven set-up steps to the first production send. **Settings → Set-Up** is the page that walks them, read from the workspace so it always knows what is done. The chapters for the pages it opens come next: Users and Roles, Sending Engines, Suppressions, Contact Lists, Notifications and Approvals.

# Home

**Where:** Home in the main navigation, and the C360 mark at the top of the rail.

## What this page is for

Home is the marketing command centre: how the workspace's campaigns are doing, what needs a decision, and the quickest way to start something new. Every figure on it is read live from the same reports and campaigns the other pages show, filtered by the campaigns, channel and period you choose at the top of Marketing Performance. Nothing is entered on Home; it is the place to look first in the morning.

## Who can use it

| | |
|---|---|
| Permission | Everyone who can sign in sees Home. Each card needs the permission of what it reads: the figures, Marketing Performance, Top Campaigns, Governance and the funnel need `report.view`; Needs Attention and Recent Campaigns need `campaign.view`; the set-up card is for administrators (`user.admin`). A card the viewer may not read is left out and the rest close the gap. |
| Features | Active Journeys needs the **journeys** feature; E-Receipts needs **ereceipt**; the SMS and MMS choices in the channel filter need **sms** and **mms**. |

## The fields and controls

|---|---|---|

## How to

**See one campaign's picture.** Pick it under Campaigns. The figures, the revenue chart and the funnel read that campaign alone, and Needs Attention shows only its own alerts.

**Compare this month with last.** Set Period to Last 30 Days. Every figure's change chip is against the 30 days before.

**Act on an alert.** Press the card's link: Review Failures, Publish or Review. The campaign list opens already filtered to those campaigns.

**Start something.** Create New → Campaign opens the Create Campaign pop-up; Template opens New Template; Contact List opens the wizard.

## What happens behind the scenes

1. Home asks for the period's report summary and the one before it, the overview (alerts, revenue and cost, governance, journeys) in one request, the calendar for the week ahead and the recently changed campaigns. The summary and the overview are kept for 30 seconds on the server, so a second look within that time is cheap.
2. A request the viewer may not make hides its card instead of showing an error; a request that fails shows the card with Try Again.
3. The figures are the report's figures: the same numbers as Reports and each campaign's Performance tab, so a number on Home can always be traced to its campaign.

## Things to know

- Home is a reading, not a ledger: figures lag by up to 30 seconds and e-receipts are kept apart from marketing figures.
- Revenue, conversions and ROI come from the orders the shop sends, credited to campaigns every five minutes (see Attribution). Until attribution is on they read zero or *not set up*.
- The page keeps your filters only while you stay on it; the next visit starts at every campaign, all channels, the last 30 days.

# Contact Lists

**Where:** Contact Lists in the main navigation, and **Contact Lists → Uploads**.

## What this page is for

A contact list is a reusable audience: the people a campaign is sent to. Lists come from a file you upload, from an audience your data team defined in the BI tool, or from a copy of another list. A campaign never reads a list while it sends: when a run starts, the attached lists are merged into the campaign's own frozen copy, so you can change a list at any time without changing a send that was already built.

The page shows every list of the workspace with its size and where it is used, and starts the wizard that creates one.

## Who can use it

| | |
|---|---|
| Permission | `list.view` to see lists and their people; `list.edit` to create, edit, clone, archive and delete; `list.import` to upload a file; `consent.edit` to approve or refuse a list that waits for approval. |
| Features | Always on. A list *for texts* only matters when the workspace has the **sms** feature. |

### The page

| Control | What it is | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| Total Lists, Total Contacts, Attached to Live Campaigns | The counts for the workspace: lists, distinct people across them, and lists attached to a campaign that is published to production or sending now. | Nothing; a reading. |
| Search | Filters the table by name as you type. | |
| Type | Production or Test. | A campaign published to production refuses a Test list. Keep real sends and rehearsals apart with it. |
| Source | Upload, BI or Extraction, Cloned. | |
| Uploads | Opens the Uploads page: every file upload of the workspace with its progress, who started it, Continue, Cancel and its report. | |
| Create List | Opens the wizard. | |
| The table | Name and description; **For** (Email, Texts, Email and Texts); the number of people; the source; the type; how many campaigns it is attached to and how many of those are live; when it changed. A note under the name when the list waits for approval or was refused. | |
| Row menu | **Open** the list, **Clone** it (asks for the copy's name), **Delete** it. | Delete is refused while the list is attached to a campaign: archive it from its page instead. Deleting removes its people from the list, never from C360. |

| | Type | Production or Test. | See Type above. |

### The Uploads page

| Control | What it is | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| The list of uploads | Newest first: file, list, who started it, status and progress. | |
| Continue | Opens the wizard again at the step an upload is waiting on (matching, consent, review). | |

The upload button in the header shows the uploads running on every page and raises an alert when one finishes, fails, or waits for a review.

## How to

**Upload a file.** Contact Lists → Create List → Upload a File → name the list, say what it is for, choose Production or Test, add the file. Match the columns and confirm. Say where the people agreed and confirm the attestation. Read the review, then Import. The list is a draft until the import is applied; leaving the wizard before that deletes the draft.

**Prepare a file that imports cleanly.** Use Download Sample CSV as the model: a header row, one person per row, dates as `YYYY-MM-DD`, mobile numbers with the country code (`+14045550101`). Put an email consent date and a text consent date in for everyone who gave one: without a date, a new person has no consent on record and marketing sends leave them out.

**Keep a do-not-contact list first.** Before the first upload, import the people who opted out elsewhere under Settings → Suppressions. Then no upload can ever reach them.

**Approve a list that waits.** When the workspace's Consent Policy asks for approval before the first send, a fresh upload sets its list to *awaiting approval* and every campaign holds its people back as *in a list awaiting approval*. Someone with `consent.edit` opens the list and presses Approve, or Refuse with a note.

**Reuse a list on several campaigns.** Attach it in each campaign's Send To step. The list's page names every campaign it is on, and warns before an edit when one of them is live.

## What happens behind the scenes

1. **The file is stored and queued**, never processed inside your request. Reading, checking and importing run in the background, in chunks, at most a few uploads at a time across the whole platform and never two of the same workspace at once, so one large file cannot slow everyone down. The page keeps showing progress; a hidden browser tab stops asking.
2. **Reading** stages every row and guesses the columns from the headers.
3. **Checking** holds back rows without a usable address, rows whose address appears earlier in the file, and, under a strict policy, rows without a consent date. Everyone else is matched to a person C360 knows (by loyalty ID first, then by address or number) or marked as new. Each importable row is judged exactly as a marketing send would judge that person today, with the send engine's own rules, so a word means the same at upload and in a campaign's results.
4. **The declaration** is recorded: the basis, where it was collected, who attested and when.
5. **Importing** creates the new people, adds everyone with a usable address to the list (whatever their status: people resubscribe, addresses get unblocked, and the send engine decides again for real at every run), writes an opt-in for a consent date only where C360 had no answer, and never overrides an unsubscribe. The list becomes active; under an approval policy it waits for approval first.
6. **The compliance record** on the list's page shows, for as long as the list exists: the source, every upload with its declaration, the approval, the policy as it stands today and today's consent by channel.

## Things to know

- Uploading someone does not give consent. Only a consent date in the file, a sign-up through a C360 form or a preference page, or a record from your other systems does.
- The review's numbers add up: rows in the file = held back + ready; ready = added + already in the list. The row-by-row report is the audit trail of an upload.
- Lists are never deleted while a campaign uses them; archive instead. People are never deleted from C360 by a list action.
- A Test list cannot go to production. Make the real list a Production one from the start, or clone it.
- Excel files are not accepted; save as CSV first.

## The customer page

**Where:** **Find a Customer** on the Contact Lists page (`/customers`); a customer's name on a journey's Customers card, on Settings → Events and on a list's members opens their page directly.

One customer across the workspace, read from the records C360 syncs (or an upload added). Nothing is edited here: the record is C360's.

| Part | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Header | The name, the customer id, where the record came from and when it was last updated, and a tag per channel saying whether they can receive marketing (Email: Can receive; SMS: No consent). |
| Record | Email addresses, phone numbers and the address, as the record holds them. |
| Consent | Per channel: the consent status, whether they can receive and why not, when they opted in or out and how, and any suppression. This is the verdict the send engine uses. |
| Custom Attributes | Every field C360 sent with the record (loyalty tier, home store, time zone, dates), which conditions, splits, date entries and time-of-day waits read. |
| Journeys | Every journey they entered, with where they are now, whether they converted and **View Timeline**, which opens their timeline on the journey's page; and the journeys they are kept out of by a suppression. |
| Events | The last 50 events received for them, with their data. |
| Orders | The last 20 orders Attribution received, and the campaign each was credited to. |
| Messages | The last 30 messages campaigns and journeys sent them, with what became of each (delivered, opened, clicked, or why it was not sent). |
| Contact Lists | The lists they are on and how they got there. Lists a journey makes for its own sends are left out. |

**Finding a customer.** Type part of a name, an email address, a mobile number (digits only are fine) or a customer id. A customer who is not in the records does not appear: C360 has to sync them, or an upload has to add them, before C360 can reach them.

Who may see it: anyone who may see contact lists, campaigns or journeys.

# Campaigns

**Where:** Campaigns in the main navigation; a campaign's own page at Campaigns → the campaign.

## What this page is for

A campaign is what is sent, to whom, when and why. Every actual send is a run of its campaign against a copy of its lists frozen when the run starts, so a recurring or drip campaign stays one campaign with many runs and its results roll up. The Campaigns page lists and plans them, as a table or a calendar; a campaign's page shows everything about one campaign: its status and the actions it allows, its performance, its audience, its content, and what every recipient's message became.

Creating and editing a campaign is the Campaign Set-Up, which has its own chapter.

## Who can use it

| | |
|---|---|
| Permission | `campaign.view` to see campaigns and their results; `campaign.edit` to create, edit, clone and archive; `campaign.publish` to publish to production, pause, resume and cancel a schedule; `campaign.approve` to approve a campaign when the workspace asks for approvals. |
| Features | Texts need **sms** (and **mms** for picture messages), e-receipt campaigns need **ereceipt**, subject line tests need **ab_testing**, journey-generated campaigns need **journeys**. |

## The fields and controls

### The Campaigns page

| Control | What it is | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| Create Campaign | Opens the Create Campaign pop-up (see Campaign Set-Up). | |
| Upcoming campaigns | The next four scheduled sends: channel, name, the date and the time in the campaign's own time zone, recipients. | A send opens its campaign. |
| List View / Calendar View | The table, or the month with every campaign as a bar over the days it runs. | Remembered in the address, so a link keeps the view. |
| Saved views | All, Mine, Sending Today, Needs Attention, Awaiting Approval (shown to approvers and publishers), Drafts. | One click sets the filters below to that question. |
| Search | By name or code. | |
| Filters | Triggered By (manual, journey, and the owner), Status, Type, Channel, Date Created (a preset or a custom range), Tag. | The table and the calendar alike. In the calendar the month replaces the date range. |

## How to

**Find what needs you.** The Needs Attention saved view lists failed, waiting and unapproved campaigns; Awaiting Approval lists what approvers must decide; Drafts lists what is unfinished.

**Pause a send that is going wrong.** Pause on the row or the page. Messages the engine already has still go out; everything queued waits until Resume.

**Stop a scheduled send.** Cancel Schedule. The campaign returns to an editable draft.

**Reuse a campaign.** Clone. Rename the copy, check its lists and schedule, publish.

**Pause, resume or stop a drip.** Pause holds every waiting message. Resume shows first what happens to the messages whose day passed while paused (they go at the next send time, one a day, and later ones move only if they must). Stop Remaining Messages cancels the rest for good; the drip is Completed, stopped early, and its templates are free again. To send it again, clone it.

**Retry a drip message that failed.** When a message failed and nobody received it, its card and banner offer Retry Message: it goes again now to the same audience.

**Approve a campaign.** When the workspace's Approvals setting is on, a campaign asks for approval on its Review & Publish step; an approver sees it under Awaiting Approval, opens it and approves or sends it back with a note. The person who asked can never be the one who approves.

**Read what happened to one person's message.** Recipients → search the address → open the row. The timeline shows when it was queued, handed to the engine, and every delivery, bounce, open and click the engine reported; Check with the Engine fetches the engine's own record.

## What happens behind the scenes

1. **Publishing** runs every check (a published template with content, an opt-out link in a marketing email, a verified sender, at least one production list, a time in the future for a schedule) and creates the run or runs: one for a one-time send, one per occurrence for a recurrence as they come due, one per step for a drip series.
2. **At the run's time** the dispatcher, which looks every minute, starts the run: the included lists are merged into the campaign's own frozen list, one entry per person, the excluded lists' people marked, and governance applied in order: bounced, complained or blocked addresses, invalid addresses, the suppression list, unsubscribes, consent, the campaign's topic, frequency limits, duplicates. Transactional, service and legal campaigns skip the consent rules but never a blocked or invalid address.
3. **Sending** hands the messages to the sender's engine in batches; a subject line test splits a sample between A and B and holds the rest until the winner is chosen; recipient-local sending holds each message until the chosen time in the person's own zone.
4. **The engine calls back** and every message's row moves forward: delivered, opened, clicked, bounced, complained. The campaign's figures, the report and Home all read those rows.
5. **The activity log** records each step, and a failed run raises a banner on the campaign and an alert on Home.

## Things to know

- The frozen list is made when a run starts, not at publish: a scheduled send uses the lists as they are on the day, and governance is always decided at send time.
- A Test list can never go to production; a test publish never counts in results.
- Pause does not recall messages the engine already accepted.
- A journey-generated campaign is read-only here; change the journey instead.
- The hourly rate limit is stored and shown but not enforced yet; the engine's Messages per Minute is.

|---|---|

## How to

1. **Read a month.** Leave Period on This Month; switch Dataset to Journeys to see the journeys' side of the same month.
2. **Follow one journey.** On the Journeys dataset choose it under Journey; every tile, the curve and the CSV narrow to it.
3. **Keep the numbers.** Export CSV while the period and the filter are set; the file is named for the dataset and the period.

## What happens behind the scenes

The Campaigns dataset reads the execution tables once per channel for the period and keeps the answer for 30 seconds. The Journeys dataset reads enrollments (entered by the day they entered, converted by the day the goal was reached, with their revenue), the journeys' generated campaigns' messages (test runs left out) and the message steps that were held back or failed, in the workspace's time zone, and keeps the answer for 30 seconds as well. A journey deleted since stays out of the figures.

## Things to know

- Rates over fewer than 50 sends are left out of Best and Worst.
- The Journeys dataset counts a message on the day it was sent, an entry on the day the customer entered, and a conversion on the day the goal event arrived, so the three can fall on different days for the same customer.
- Held back is the message steps governance stopped (no consent, a suppression, a frequency cap, quiet hours); the journey page's Customers card can list the customers behind each reason.
- The weekly email carries the Campaigns dataset.

## How to

**Rehearse without sending to anyone.** Attach a Test list, send tests to colleagues, publish to test. A test publish moves the status and sends nothing to real people.

**Send a series.** Drip on Create Campaign → Audience: tick the list → Drip Campaign Setup: build message 1, then Add Message (a copy of the last keeps the header and footer), give each its day and subject, choose the start date, the send time and the zone, and the sender → Targets → Test & Publish: Send All as a Test, check each message, then Publish to Production.

**Send at everyone's 10 a.m.** Tick Send at Each Recipient's Local Time and set Send At to 10:00. The step says how many people have a known zone; the others get 10:00 in the campaign's zone.

**Test two subject lines.** On the Test step switch on Test a Second Subject Line, write B, keep the sample at 20 percent and the decision at four hours, save. The Performance tab shows A and B side by side once the run starts.

## A Drip Campaign

A drip sends several messages on a timetable to one audience on one channel. Everyone on the campaign list starts together on the start date; each message goes on its day (Day 0 is the start date) at the series time, or at its own time.

| Step | What you do there |
|---|---|
| Audience | As for any campaign. The note says how a drip audience works: people added to the lists after message 1 don't join, and consent and frequency limits are checked again before each message. |
| Drip Campaign Setup | The messages as a timeline of cards, in day order, with the gap between them. Open a card to set its name (it names the template copy too), its day, its own send time, the template (Choose from Gallery, Edit Template, Change), the subject line and preheader (email) or the text (SMS, MMS), and the A/B subject test (one message per drip, email only). Add Message starts from a copy of the last message, a template from the gallery, or a blank one; Copy as Next Message does the same from the open one; Remove Message asks first. Series at a Glance holds the start date, the send time and the time zone, what the series adds up to and a calendar; Sender (and Topic) are set once for every message. Changing the date, time or zone moves every message's date but never changes the messages. Continue lists the messages that still need something, with Show First Problem. |
| Targets | As for any campaign; the targets are for the drip as a whole. |
| Test & Publish | Preview each message (as a sample customer, on desktop or mobile). Send All as a Test sends every message now, one after another, to the people you pick; each subject starts with its place: "[Test 2 of 3 · would send Day 3]" for email, "[Test 2/3 · Day 3]" for texts, and the A/B message sends both subjects. A message edited after its test says Changed since the test; Resend it. The Review Timeline shows each message's date, subject and reach, with warnings for messages closer than two days, frequency limits and quiet hours. Before You Publish lists what's left; Publish to Production plans every message at once. |

Each message has its own copy of its template ("Loyalty Welcome Series · 2 of 4"), so editing one never changes another. From the editor, Back to Drip Campaign Setup returns to the message. A message's own subject line is what it sends; its template copy needs none.

Once published, the campaign page shows the Series Timeline: each message Sent with its figures, Sending, Next Up, Scheduled, On Hold, Cancelled or Failed, and who was skipped and why (View People lists them). Pause holds the waiting messages; Resume first shows when the missed ones will go. Stop Remaining Messages cancels the rest for good (to send again, clone it). A failed message nobody received can be retried. Clone asks for the copy's name and start date and opens its Drip Campaign Setup step.

**Not in the first version:** people can't join a drip after it starts (no rolling entry), conversions and revenue aren't split by message, there's no view of where one person is in the series, test recipients can't be saved as a list, and a change can't be applied to every message at once.

## What happens behind the scenes

1. Continue validates the step and saves the draft; the API's field messages land on the same fields and the step that holds the first one opens. A save against an older version is refused and the page offers to reload.
2. The set-up remembers how far it got: an unfinished draft opens at the next step and the steps ahead stay closed, so the publish cannot be reached early.
3. The audience estimate runs the send engine's own governance order as of today; the frozen list at the run is the record of what a send used.
4. Production Publish runs the checklist, records who published, asks for approval when the workspace requires it, and creates the runs the dispatcher will start on time.

## Things to know

- Type and channel are fixed once the campaign exists; make a new campaign to change them.
- A Test list blocks a production publish; an unverified sender blocks it too, while test sends still work.
- A campaign's own subject line never changes the template; other campaigns on the same template keep theirs.
- The hourly limit is not enforced yet; pace a send with the engine's Messages per Minute.
- A drip's messages can't change once it is published, and their templates are locked (read-only, not deletable) until it finishes or is stopped.

| Presentation | Compact, Standard, Itemized, Store Register, or Custom HTML. | The shape of the receipt inside the email. |
| Receipt Sections | Order header, Customer & fulfillment, Items, Totals, Payment, Loyalty, Returns & support: each on or off (some are fixed), each with its own switches: the order number, date, store and receipt ID; the address shown; the item columns and row height; the totals rows and a savings callout; the payment method, always masked to the last four digits; points and tier progress; the policy text and an in-store return barcode. | What the point of sale's transaction is laid out as, at send time. |

## How to

**Make an email from a preset.** Templates → scroll to Starting Point → open a preset → Copy This Template → name it → the designer opens with the copy; change the text and images, Save, Activate.

**Personalize.** Put the cursor in a text block, open Tags, pick First Name. The canvas shows the sample value; each recipient gets their own.

**Add a coupon with a barcode.** Commerce → Barcode; in the settings pick the source tag (for example the coupon code uploaded with the list) and the format. The sent email carries each recipient's own code; a recipient without a value gets the email without the block.

**Hide a block on phones.** Select it → Hide on Phones. The phone view of the canvas shows the result.

**Go back to an earlier version.** History → open the version → Save. It becomes the newest version; nothing is lost.

**Write a text that fits in one part.** Keep under 160 characters without emoji; the counter says how many parts the text will be sent as.

## What happens behind the scenes

1. The designer saves the design twice over: the editor's own state, to reopen exactly as left, and the MJML source. The server renders the HTML from the MJML, derives the text part, and checks the design (tags, subject, unsubscribe link, size). The browser never supplies the final HTML.
2. Every save is a new version in the template's history; Activate marks the template usable by campaigns.
3. At send time the engine fills the tags from the workspace's tag catalogue, the recipient's list columns and the customer record, draws each barcode and QR code, puts the campaign's subject and preheader in when it has its own, and for a receipt lays out the transaction the point of sale sent into the receipt slot.
4. Images are uploaded once per workspace and served from the media store, never embedded in the email.

## Things to know

- Changing a template changes what every campaign using it sends from then on. Duplicate when a campaign must keep its own version.
- A drip gives every message its own copy of a template, so a drip's messages never change other campaigns. Opened from the Drip Campaign Setup step the editor says which message it is, keeps the subject on the message (read-only here), and leads Back to Drip Campaign Setup. While the drip is live the copy is locked: the preview and the editor say "Used by a live drip (…). It can't be changed until the drip finishes or is stopped." and open read-only.
- Only an active template can be published; a draft can be previewed and test sent.
- A marketing email must carry the unsubscribe link; the designer blocks Activate without it and the campaign cannot publish.
- Outlook for Windows ignores shadows; the field says so.
- A text template cannot have a subject line; a campaign on it cannot either.

## Deliverability mode

## Saving, versions and activating

- **Save** writes version n+1. The designer also saves a draft by itself 30 seconds after the last edit, unless the template is read-only, an older version is on the canvas, or the last save was refused.
- **History** lists every version with who saved it and when. Open one to look at it: the strip says *You are looking at version n*; saving from there makes it the newest version, nothing is lost; Back to Latest returns.
- **A conflict**: when someone else saved a newer version, the strip says so and offers Load Latest Version. Nothing is sent until you do.
- **Activate** runs the blockers: a subject line, the design saved at least once, an unsubscribe link for marketing mail, images with an alt text and no placeholder images, the HTML under Gmail's 102 KB clip limit. A template with an error cannot be activated; warnings are advisory. Once active, campaigns can pick it on their Content step and journeys on a message step.
- **Leaving** with unsaved changes asks first.

## How to use the editor: worked examples

Each example is a complete recipe. Names, colours and addresses are placeholders for your own.

### 1. A welcome email from a starter

1. Templates → New Template → Email → name it *Welcome to Northwind* → Starting Point: Welcome email → Create.
2. Subject: type *Welcome,* then insert `first_name` from the tag picker beside the field; the counter stays green. Preheader: *Your first order ships free this month.*
3. Select the Header block → Wordmark Text: Northwind Apparel → Brand Bar Color: your navy.
4. Double-click the hero text → write the welcome in two short sentences; keep the heading one line.
5. Select the Button → Label: Start Shopping → Link: your shop's address; Appearance → Background Color: your action colour.
6. Preview → Client: Gmail → check the inbox row reads well where Gmail cuts it; Device: Mobile → check the button is reachable with a thumb.
7. Send a Test to yourself, then Save and Activate. The campaign's Content step now offers it.

### 2. Formatting text, links and emoji

1. Double-click a Text block. The toolbar appears above it: **bold**, *italic*, underline, strikethrough, link, tag, emoji.
2. Select a phrase and press the link button → paste the address. Select the link again: the settings panel now shows **Link Color** and **Underline** for that link alone, so a link can be your brand blue without an underline while the paragraph stays black.
3. Press the tag button → pick `first_name`: it lands where the cursor is. Press the emoji button for a mark at the cursor; the emoji picker beside Subject does the same for the subject line.
4. The block's own settings set the whole block: Font Family (the email fonts the workspace offers; the default is the design's own), Font Size, Font Weight, Line Height, Text Color, Alignment. Use the toolbar for a word, the panel for the block.
5. Press Escape to finish editing. The **Links** pill at the bottom lists every link in the design with its address: open it before saving to catch a link to nowhere.

### 3. A hero: a coloured section, a picture and a button

1. Drag **One Column** (Structure) below the header. Select the section (the breadcrumb above the canvas shows section → column → block; click *section*).
2. Appearance → Background Color: your brand colour; Layout → Full Width on, so the colour runs edge to edge in inboxes that allow it; Padding: 32 px top and bottom.
3. Drag an **Image** into the column → Assets → upload the hero picture → Alt Text: what it shows (*A rack of autumn coats*). Width: 100%; Corner Radius: 8 px.
4. Drag a **Heading** under the image → Text Color: white; then a **Button** → Label: Shop the Collection → Link; Corner Radius: 8 px; Margin 16 px on top to lift it off the heading.
5. Switch the canvas to **Mobile**: the picture scales to the column; if it is too tall, set **Max Height** on the image. Save.

### 4. Columns that behave on a phone

1. Drag **Two Columns** → left column an Image, right column a Heading and a Text. On the desktop canvas they sit side by side.
2. Switch to **Mobile**. Columns stack, picture first. If the words should come first on a phone, select the section → Layout → **On Phones: reverse**.
3. Two small items that should stay beside each other on a phone (two icons, a price and a button): select their section → **Keep Side by Side**. They share the width instead of stacking.
4. A **Three Columns** section with a Product Card in each stacks to three cards on a phone; if that is too long, select the third card → Layout → **Hide on Phones**. The desktop email keeps all three.
5. The other way round: a square banner that only phones should see → set its section to **Hide on Desktop**. The canvas hides it on the device it does not apply to; Show Block Outlines on the toolbar keeps hidden blocks findable.

### 5. Images: the library, alt text and sizes

1. Select an Image block → **Image** → the library opens: search by name, pick one, or Upload (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, up to 5 MB; a GIF animates in inboxes that play them, Outlook shows its first frame). The same file uploaded twice is kept once, so re-uploading is harmless.
2. **Alt Text** is read by people who cannot see the picture and shown where images are blocked; a picture without one is a finding, and a placeholder picture blocks Activate.
3. **Link** makes the picture clickable; **Hover Text** is the tooltip. **Width** in %, **Max Height** to keep tall pictures in check, **Corner Radius** for rounded corners, **Padding** for breathing room.
4. Give photos a 2:1 or 3:2 shape; a 600 px wide export is plenty for a 680 px email and keeps the message under Gmail's clip limit.

### 6. A sale with a personal coupon and a barcode

1. New Template → Email → Starting Point: Sale announcement.
2. Drag a **Coupon** block under the hero. Headline: *20% off this weekend*; Body: insert the `coupon_code` tag from the quick tags, so each person sees their own code; Treatment: dashed.
3. Drag a **Barcode** block under the coupon. Source: `coupon_barcode_value`; Format: Code 128; the value under the code on. The canvas shows the sample value's bars; the sent email draws each recipient's.
4. Upload the contact list with columns named `coupon_code` and `coupon_barcode_value`; the list's people carry them.
5. A recipient with no code gets the email without the barcode block; the run's completion message says how many.
6. Preview As → Empty Profile shows what a person without a code sees. Save, Activate.

### 7. A QR code for in-store redemption

1. Drag a **QR Code** block (Commerce; offered when the workspace has Barcodes and QR codes). Source: `coupon_qr_value`; Error Correction: Standard (High if the code will be printed small or on a dark background); Size: 160 px; Alignment: centre.
2. Give the QR value in the list upload a full URL or the till's code, whatever the store scans.
3. On a dark section, set the block's **Background** to white: a QR code needs light around it to scan.

### 8. Social links, including TikTok

1. Drag **Social Links** (Brand) into the footer section. **Add a Network** → Network: Instagram → Link: your profile. Add Facebook, TikTok, YouTube the same way; each row has its own Link and an optional Label.
2. Layout: a row for a footer, a column for a sidebar. Icon Size 32 px, Icon Background your brand colour or none, Icon Corner Radius 16 px for round icons; Label Font, Label Size, Label Color when labels are shown. The TikTok mark takes the colour its neighbours have.

### 9. Something the blocks cannot do: Custom HTML

1. Drag **Custom HTML** (Content) where it should go: into a column, a section or the body. It refuses to land inside another raw block.
2. HTML: write an email-safe table, for example a two-row opening-hours table with inline styles. No scripts and no external stylesheets: the server rejects scripts, and inboxes ignore stylesheets.
3. Layout: Width (full or a fixed width), Alignment, Margin and Padding, Background Color: these wrap your HTML in a table the inboxes understand, so the block lines up with the rest. Hide on Phones and Hide on Desktop work on it too.
4. Preview in Outlook as well as Gmail: raw HTML is where the two differ most.

### 10. Spacing with rhythm

1. Put a **Spacer** (Structure) between sections and tune its Height once, rather than padding every block: one place to change the rhythm.
2. On a block, **Margin** is the space outside it (between it and its neighbours), **Padding** the space inside (between its edge and its content). A Button is the one block where Padding is the space inside the button itself.
3. The four boxes take px or %, and em or rem if you think that way: 1.5em becomes px the moment you press Enter, so the email stays predictable in every inbox. Mixing px and % on one side is refused.

### 11. Borders and shadows

1. Select a section, column, image or button → Appearance → Border Width 1 px, Border Style solid, Border Color a light grey: a hairline card. Corner Radius rounds it.
2. Shadow: soft, medium or strong on a section, column, text, image or button. Apple Mail, Gmail and Outlook.com draw it; Outlook for Windows drops shadows, so never let a shadow carry meaning.

### 12. The subject line and preheader

1. Write the subject as a sentence, under 60 characters: the counter turns amber past that, where inboxes cut it. Insert `first_name` from the picker for a personal subject, an emoji from the picker beside it if your brand uses them.
2. A hint appears under the field when a word is one spam filters weigh (*free*, *act now*, capitals, stacked punctuation). It is advice, not a verdict.
3. The preheader is the line inboxes show after the subject: write the second half of the thought, under 90 characters. Leave it empty and inboxes show the first words of the email instead.
4. Preview → Client: Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail: the inbox row above the email shows where each cuts the two.

### 13. Checking the email in Preview

1. Switch to **Preview**. Preview As: Sample Values first, then **Empty Profile**: every tag shows its default, which is what a customer with a thin record sees. Fix a default under Settings → Tags.
2. Device: Mobile. Everything should be readable without zooming; buttons wide enough for a thumb.
3. Client: Outlook. Shadows are gone, rounded corners square off; the design should still hold.
4. Appearance: Dark. A dark header with dark text, or a logo with a transparent background on a dark bar, shows here before a customer sees it.
5. **Send a Test** to yourself and a colleague: the real email through the workspace's sender, as the previewed profile.

### 14. Inbox previews in real clients

1. Save, then **Deliverability → Run Inbox Previews**. The last saved version is rendered in real clients through Mailgun Inspect.
2. The result lists renders grouped by kind (desktop, phone, webmail); open one full size. Each test counts against the plan's monthly number unless the workspace uses its own Inspect account.

### 15. Versions: going back, and two people at once

1. **History** at the top right lists every version with who saved it and when. Open one to look at it; the strip says *You are looking at version n*. **Save** from there makes it the newest version, so a design from last month is one Save away. **Back to Latest** returns without saving.
2. When a colleague saves while you are editing, the strip says a newer version exists and offers **Load Latest Version**. Your unsaved work is lost by loading it, so copy any text you wrote first. Nothing is sent until the conflict is resolved.
3. The designer saves a draft by itself 30 seconds after the last edit; the footer says when it last saved.

### 16. Handing the design to a campaign or a journey

1. **Activate**. The footer names anything that blocks it: an empty subject, a placeholder image, an image without alt text, a marketing email without the unsubscribe link, an email over Gmail's 102 KB limit. Fix and Activate again.
2. A campaign picks the template on its Content step and may write its own subject and preheader without touching the template. A journey's Email step picks it in the step's panel.
3. Changing an active template changes what campaigns send from the next save that is activated; the campaigns themselves need no edit.

### 17. Taking the design elsewhere

1. **View Code** on the canvas toolbar shows the MJML source and the rendered HTML, formatted, with a copy button: for an agency, another tool, or a quick look at what an inbox will receive.
2. **Download HTML** saves the rendered email as a file.
3. **Fullscreen** hides the rails for a clean look at the canvas; **Exit Fullscreen** brings them back. **Fit to Width** and the zoom menu size the canvas to the window.

### 18. Finding and fixing a buried block

1. Open **Layers**: the tree of sections, columns and blocks with their names. Click one to select it on the canvas; rename it so the tree reads like the email; drag to reorder; the eye hides it on the canvas while you work (it still sends).
2. The breadcrumb above the canvas walks up from a block to its column and section, which is the quickest way to select a section to change its background or stacking.
3. The **Errors** pill lists every finding with a link to the field or block concerned.

## Things to know

- The subject and preheader on the template are defaults: a campaign can write its own on its Content step without touching the template.
- Marketing mail must carry the unsubscribe link; the Footer block has it. Remove it and Activate refuses, and a marketing campaign cannot publish.
- Images are stored once per workspace and served from the media store; the same file uploaded twice is kept once.
- Email clients differ: Outlook for Windows has no shadows and no media queries (the phone rules use its own mechanism), Gmail clips emails over 102 KB. The Preview's clients show the common cases; Deliverability shows the real ones.
- A design made outside C360 can be pasted as Custom HTML, but the blocks cannot edit what is inside it; rebuild with blocks where you will want to change it later.
- A text template has no designer: SMS and MMS open the message editor, e-receipts the receipt editor.

# Setting Up a New Workspace

**Where:** Settings → Workspace → Set-Up. The first sign-in of an administrator lands here while the workspace is not ready, and Home keeps a card pointing back until it is.

## What this is

The platform team creates the workspace and sends its first administrator an email with a one-time password link and the set-up list. From that email to the first production send is one path, and this chapter walks it: choosing a password, the first sign-in, the Set-Up page that knows what is done, the seven steps in order, a rehearsal with a test campaign, and the first real send. Nothing in it is a new form: every step opens the Settings page that does it, and those pages have their own chapters.

## Who does it

| | |
|---|---|
| Permission | The first administrator holds the Workspace Admin role, which has every permission including `user.admin`. Anyone with `user.admin` sees the Set-Up page. |
| Features | Step 5 (texts) reads as not available without the **sms** feature; the API Keys link under step 7 needs **api_access**. The platform team switches features on per workspace. |

## The path, from the email

### Before the first sign-in

1. **The email.** It names the workspace and its code (for example `NWA`), carries the password link and lists the set-up steps. The link is one-time: if it was used or has expired, ask the platform team, or any administrator who already has access, for a new one (Settings → Users → Password Link).
2. **Choose a password.** The link opens the password page; choose one and continue to the sign-in.
3. **Sign in.** The workspace code, your email and the password. When the workspace requires multi-factor authentication, the next screen walks you through it before anything else: scan the code with an authenticator app, type the six digits, store the recovery codes.
4. **You land on Set-Up.** An administrator whose workspace is not ready lands here instead of Home: *Welcome, {name}. Let's set up {CODE}.* with step 1 already done. Everyone else, and every later sign-in, lands on Home, where a card says how far the set-up is until the workspace is ready.

### The seven steps, in order

| 2 | Invite your team and give them roles | Users → Add User: email, name, a role. Three roles are ready: Workspace Admin, Campaign Manager, Analyst; Roles adds narrower ones, for example an approver. Each person gets their own password link. | At least one other active user with a role. |

# Sending Engines and Senders

## What this page is for

**Connect an email engine.** Add Engine → choose the type → name it → paste the address and the key → Add Engine. Press **Check** on its row and wait for *Checked*. Then **Copy Webhook URL** and register that address with the provider (Postal: the mail server's Webhooks; SendGrid: Mail settings → Event Webhook). Within a day the Callbacks column should show callbacks arriving.

**Add and verify a sender.** Add Sender → pick the engine → the from name and address → tick Default if it is the usual one → Add Sender. Press **Verify** and look for the message in your own inbox: when the engine accepts it, the status reads Verified. For texts, Verify asks for a mobile number to text.

**Keep receipts out of the campaign queue (Postal).** Edit the engine and enter the API key of a second Postal mail server as the Transactional Server API Key. Receipts and transactional campaigns then use it in their own lane. One engine, one callback address, two keys.

**Replace a leaked key.** Edit the engine, type the new key over the stored one, save, press Check. For the callback address, press **Rotate Address** and register the new address with the provider; the old one answers nothing from the moment you rotate.

**Try texts without an aggregator.** Add the Sandbox engine and a sender on it. Text campaigns then run end to end and nothing is sent. The Sandbox can even play back deliveries and STOP replies when a developer posts to its callback address.

## What happens behind the scenes

1. **Publishing** a campaign attaches the sender you chose; the sender names the engine.
2. **At the run's time** C360 builds the audience, leaves out the people it must (no consent, unsubscribed, bounced, over the frequency cap, and so on), and hands the messages to the engine in batches, paced by Messages per Minute.
3. **The engine answers** per message: accepted (C360 records the engine's message id), rejected (failed, never retried) or try again (retried after 1, 5, 15, 30 and 60 minutes; after five attempts it fails). A refused key or a suspended account stops the whole run with that reason.
4. **The engine calls back** to the callback address as things happen: delivered, bounced, deferred, dropped, complained, opened, clicked, unsubscribed. C360 keeps every callback and moves the message's status forward. A hard bounce or a complaint blocks the address for every future campaign; a complaint or an unsubscribe also records an opt-out.
5. **Texts** follow the same path with their own vocabulary: delivered or failed receipts, and replies. A STOP reply opts the number out of every kind of campaign and blocks it; START lifts the block.

The campaign's **Recipients** tab shows each message's story, and **Check with the Engine** on a row asks Postal directly what became of it.

## Things to know

- A sent message with no delivered time, and *No callback has ever arrived* on the engine, means the callback address is not registered with the provider. The send worked; the news is not coming back.
- Verification is per address. Changing a sender's From Address resets it to Not Verified Yet.
- Removing an engine removes its senders. Switch it off instead when you may need it again.
- Keys are stored encrypted and are never shown, exported or written to the audit trail.
- The platform's own mail server (used for password links and notifications) is never a campaign engine. Your workspace's own mail server can be, from Settings → Email Server.

# Attribution

**Where:** Settings → Integrations → Attribution.

## What is attribution

Attribution answers one question: **which campaign earned this order?** The shop sends C360 its orders (and the point of sale its store sales, if you choose). C360 matches each order to the customer, looks back at what it sent that customer, and credits the order to one campaign. Adding those orders up gives each campaign its **revenue**, and with its cost, its **ROI**.

C360 credits an order to the first of these that applies:

1. **A campaign code.** The order carries the campaign's code (for example CMP-2026-0042), as `campaign_code` or as one of its coupon codes. That campaign gets it, whatever else happened.
2. **The latest click** on a campaign's link by that customer within the **Click Window** (7 days by default).
3. **The latest open** of a marketing email within the **Open Window** (1 day by default).
4. **The latest text** delivered to that customer within the **Text Window** (1 day by default). Text links are not tracked yet, so delivery stands in for a click.

One campaign gets the whole amount (this is called *last touch*). An order that matches none of these is **Not Credited**, and the page says why. A refund follows its original order and takes the money back from the same campaign.

Until attribution is on, every revenue figure in C360 reads *Not Set Up*: Home's Attributed Revenue, Campaign ROI, Marketing Performance and Top Campaigns, each campaign's revenue against its target, the Reports revenue column, and the e-receipts' Revenue from Receipt Offers.

## Who can use it

| | |
|---|---|
| Permission | Read with `report.view`; changed with `user.admin`. The shop sends orders with an API key that has the scope `orders:ingest`. |

## How to configure attribution

**1. Make a key for the shop.** Settings → API Keys → New Key, with the scope `orders:ingest`. Copy it: it is shown once.

**2. Switch it on.** Settings → Attribution → **Attribution** on. From now on the shop's orders are accepted (while it is off they are refused with 409), and every campaign that has ever sent gets figures within five minutes, 0 until its first order.

**3. Choose the windows.** Each saves as you change it.

| Setting | Choices | Means |
|---|---|---|
| Click Window | 1, 3, 7, 14 or 30 days | How long after a click the click still earns an order. |
| Open Window | Opens Do Not Count, 1, 2, 3 or 7 days | How long an open of a marketing email earns an order when there was no click. Scanner opens and receipts never count. |
| Text Window | Texts Do Not Count, 1, 2, 3 or 7 days | How long a delivered marketing text earns an order when there was no click or open. |

**4. Count store sales** (shown when the workspace has POS Receipts). Switch **Count Store Sales** on and every transaction the point of sale posts also becomes an order: the amount without tax and shipping, the customer, the store and its promotion codes. Returns become refunds; employee sales are left out.

**5. Enter the send costs.** **Send Costs**: what 1,000 delivered emails and 1,000 delivered texts cost you, then **Save Costs**. A campaign's cost is then its delivered messages at these rates plus its offer cost, and ROI, contribution and cost per conversion follow. Left empty, ROI uses each campaign's planned cost.

**6. Connect the shop.** Give the shop's developer the endpoint, the headers and the example under **Send Orders from the Shop**:

```
POST /api/v1/orders/
Authorization: Bearer <the API key>
X-Tenant: <the workspace code>

{"order_id": "W-100235", "amount": 129.50, "currency": "USD",
 "email": "jane@example.com", "ordered_at": "2026-10-04T14:30:00Z",
 "coupon_codes": ["FALL20"], "channel": "ONLINE"}
```

Each order needs `order_id` and `amount` (revenue to count, 0 or more) and names the customer by `party_id`, `email` or `phone`. Optional: `ordered_at` (defaults to now), `currency`, `channel` (ONLINE, STORE, APP, PHONE or OTHER), `campaign_code`, `coupon_codes`, `store_number`, `items`. A refund is `"type": "REFUND"` with the positive amount and `original_order_id`. Up to 500 at once as `{"orders": [...]}`.

## How to use it in real time

### What happens to an order, minute by minute

| When | What you see |
|---|---|
| The shop posts the order | The answer says Accepted, Duplicate (the same order was already received; it counts once) or Rejected with the field at fault. Every call is in Settings → Integrations' call log. |
| Within a minute | The order is in **Latest Orders** as **Waiting**. It also raises a Purchase event (and First Purchase for a customer's first order), so a journey that starts on a purchase enrols the customer within a minute. |
| One to five minutes later | The attribute-orders job credits it: **Credited** with the campaign and how (Campaign Code, Click, Open, Text), or **Not Credited** with the reason. It waits a minute after an order arrives so a click still on its way from the sending engine is counted. |
| Within five minutes | The campaign's figures are rewritten: revenue net of refunds, orders, buyers, cost, contribution, ROI. Home, the campaign's page and Reports show them on their next refresh. |

### Check that it works: one test order

1. Pick a customer you emailed recently who clicked the email (your own test address is best).
2. Post one order for them with the shop's key, using their email address and a small amount, for example with Postman or curl.
3. Within a minute it is in **Latest Orders** as Waiting; within five it reads **Credited** with that campaign and **Click**.
4. Open the campaign: its revenue now includes the amount. Home's Attributed Revenue includes it too.
5. Post the same order again: the answer says Duplicate, and nothing is counted twice. To remove the test amount, post a refund for it (`"type": "REFUND"`, `original_order_id`).

### Watch the orders as they come in

**Latest Orders** shows the newest 50: when, the order, the amount, and the campaign credited with how, or why none. Filter **State** by **Credited**, **Not Credited** or **Waiting**; **Search** by order, email or campaign.

**Last 30 Days** sums them up: orders, revenue net of refunds, the revenue credited and the share of orders credited, orders not credited and waiting, credit by Campaign Code, Click, Open and Text, and the **Top Campaigns**.

### Read the results

| Where | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Home | Attributed Revenue, Campaign ROI (revenue per $1 spent), Marketing Performance (attributed revenue and campaign cost per day or week, with the target pace), Top Campaigns. |
| A campaign's page | Its Revenue and Conversions, against the targets set in its setup. |
| Reports | The revenue column per campaign. |
| E-Receipts on Home | Revenue from Receipt Offers (store sales credited to e-receipt campaigns). |
| Journeys | Purchase and First Purchase events start journeys and answer a Condition's "Made a Purchase". |

### Day-to-day tasks

**Credit a promotion outright.** Give the campaign's code (on the campaign's page) to the shop as the coupon, or have the shop send it as `campaign_code`. An order carrying it is credited to that campaign whatever the customer clicked.

**Find out why an order earned nothing.** Latest Orders → State: **Not Credited**. The reason is under each order: no customer C360 knows, no click, open or text within the windows, or a refund whose order was not credited.

**Change a window.** It applies to new orders. **Recalculate** (in Last 30 Days) judges the last 400 days of orders again under the current windows and rewrites every campaign's figures.

**Stop counting.** Switch Attribution off. New orders are refused (409) and revenue reads *Not Set Up* again; orders already received are kept.

## What happens behind the scenes

The attribute-orders job runs every five minutes. It finds the customer: the customer id, or whoever C360 emailed at the address or texted at the number in the last 120 days. It credits the order as above, then writes each campaign's actuals: revenue net of refunds, orders, buyers (distinct people, plus orders without a person), delivery cost, contribution (revenue minus cost), ROI (revenue over cost), revenue per delivered message and cost per buyer.

## Things to know

- Last touch is easy to explain and to check against the shop, but it gives no credit to the campaigns earlier in a customer's path.
- Revenue counts on the campaign whenever the order happens, so a campaign's figures grow for as long as its clicks are in the window.
- C360 keeps the amount, the customer, the store and the codes of an order, not its items or payment.
- An order for a customer C360 never emailed or texted, and with no customer id it knows, is Not Credited: there is nobody to match.

# Journeys

**Where:** Journeys, in the main menu; a new one also from the **+ Journey** button on Home. A journey's own page opens from the list.

## What is a journey

A journey is an automated path that each customer walks on their own. You draw it once: **who enters**, then the **steps** they go through (wait, check something, send an email or a text, issue a coupon, change their record, call your system) and where the path **ends**. From then on C360 runs it for every customer who qualifies, one at a time, at their own pace.

A campaign sends one message to a list at one moment. A journey keeps working: a customer who abandons a cart this afternoon gets the reminder an hour later, a customer whose birthday is next week gets the reward seven days before it, and a new loyalty member gets the welcome series over their first two weeks, without anyone pressing Send.

### The parts of a journey

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| Entry | What makes a customer enter: membership of a **customer list**, an **event** (a purchase, an abandoned cart, No Response …), a **date or time** (a schedule, or a date on the customer's record such as a birthday), joining or leaving a **segment**, an **attribute change** (loyalty tier, preferences, address), or the customer's **record matching conditions** (Customer Status is New). Any entry can be narrowed with **Only Customers Where** filters. |
| Population | **Fixed** (who qualifies at launch, then closed), **Continuously Qualifying** or **Event Triggered** (keeps letting newcomers in). |
| Re-entry | Whether the same customer can go through again: **Never**, **After X Days**, or **Every Qualifying Event**. Nobody is ever in the same journey twice at once. |
| Steps | Wait, Condition (Yes / No), Split, Email, SMS / MMS, Issue Coupon, Update Loyalty Tier, Update Profile, Update Segment, Add to List, Remove from List, Webhook, Goal, Exit Journey. |
| Goal | A Goal step names the outcome (an event within a window of entering); a customer who reaches it is **converted**, with the revenue of the matching orders. Without a Goal step, reaching an **Exit Journey** step counts as Goal Converted. |
| Version | Every change to a published journey is a new version; one version is live at a time, and customers finish the version they started. |

### The statuses

| Status | Means |
|---|---|
| Draft | Being built. Nobody enters. |
| Test | The test run went out; waiting for approval. |
| Approved | Approved to go live at a set time; until then the schedule can be cancelled. |
| Active | Live: customers enter and move along. |
| Paused | Nobody new enters and nobody moves; everyone waits where they are until it resumes. |
| Archived | Ended for good. Its figures and history stay readable. |

## Before the first journey

Two sides have work to do once: the platform team in the back-office, and the workspace in the app.

### What the platform team does (back-office)

The platform team's own chapter, **Journeys** in the Platform Guide, has the details. In short:

1. **The feature.** Journeys is a feature of the workspace's industry profile and plan: the tenant's page → Industry and features → Journeys → On, and a plan that includes it. Without it the Journeys menu item is missing and events sent to the workspace are refused.
2. **The scheduler.** One scheduler process (cep-jobs) runs for the platform; it enrols and moves journey customers every minute, activates scheduled journeys and rolls up the figures nightly. Nothing in a journey moves while it is stopped.
3. **The encryption key** for secrets at rest, so a Webhook step's signing secret can be kept.
4. **C360's feeds** into the workspace's database: the customer records, segments and their memberships, coupons and loyalty tiers. C360 does not create customers from nothing: a journey can only reach a customer who is in the records.

### What the workspace does (Settings)

| Item | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rights | Settings → Roles | Journeys · View (`journey.view`) sees journeys; Journeys · Create and Edit (`journey.edit`) builds, tests, retries and skips steps, duplicates and archives drafts; Journeys · Activate (`journey.activate`) approves, schedules, pauses, resumes and archives live journeys; Journeys · Export (`journey.export`) saves the Customers card as CSV; Journeys · Suppress (`journey.suppress`) keeps named customers out of a journey. Workspace Admin has all five, Analyst has View, Campaign Manager has none by default. |
| Sending | Settings → Sending Engines | An email engine with a verified default sender, and a texting engine if journeys send texts. A message step with no sender is retried for an hour and then lands on Needs Attention. |
| Templates | Templates | A **published** template for every message a journey sends. A draft template fails the readiness check. |
| A test list | Contact Lists | A list marked as a test list, holding your own team, for Test Publish. |
| Consent and governance | Settings → Consent Policy, Suppressions, Preference Centre, Governance | Journey messages go through the same send engine as campaigns: no consent, a suppression, a topic the customer unticked, a frequency cap or quiet hours hold a message back, and the customer's timeline says why. |
| Attribution | Settings → Attribution | Switch it on and have the shop send orders: every order raises a Purchase event (a customer's first one also First Purchase), the Last Purchase Date and Number of Purchases fields fill, and a Goal counts revenue. |
| Events | Settings → API Keys, then Settings → Events | A key with the scope `events:ingest` for the shop, the app and the loyalty system; the Events page gives the endpoint and an example. |
| Coupons | Settings → API Keys | A key with the scope `coupons:issue` for C360, which collects and answers the Issue Coupon step's requests. |

## The data journeys run on

A journey reads and writes the customer's record and reacts to what happens to them. This is where each piece comes from.

| Data | Comes from | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| Customer records (name, emails, phones, address, source) | C360 syncs them into the workspace; a contact list upload adds the people C360 did not know (owned by C360, not a C360 copy). | Who can enter at all; the Customer fields in conditions; the Webhook payload. |
| Custom attributes (loyalty tier, customer status, home store, lifetime value, any field C360 sends) | C360, as a JSON object on the record; the upload adds `loyalty_tier`, `loyalty_id`, `last_purchase_at`, `timezone`; the Update Profile and Update Loyalty Tier steps write them. | Conditions (text, number or date by the comparison), Split by an attribute, the attribute trigger, Wait Until a date on the record. |
| `timezone` (an IANA name such as America/Chicago) | C360, or the upload template's Time zone column. | Wait Until a Time of Day and Until a Date on the Record, in the customer's own time; the workspace's zone otherwise. |
| Dates on the record (YYYY-MM-DD) such as `birth_date`, `membership_date`, `renewal_date` | C360 custom attributes. | On a Date Attribute entries, Wait Until a Date on the Record, date conditions. |
| Segments and their members | C360 (audiences the data team defined). | Enters / Exits Segment entries, Is in a Segment conditions, the Update Segment step, Who Enters on a schedule. |
| Contact lists | Uploaded, cloned or made from a segment in C360. | Customer List entries, Is in a List conditions, Add to / Remove from List steps, Who Enters on a schedule, the test list. |
| Events (purchase, abandoned cart, store visit, birthday, any type you name) | `POST /api/v1/events/` from the shop, the app, the point of sale or the loyalty system, with an `events:ingest` key; C360 raises Purchase and First Purchase from orders, Email Open/Click and SMS Response from its own tracking, No Response, Preference Update, Loyalty Tier Change and Coupon Issue itself. | Event entries, Event conditions with property filters, the Goal step. |
| Orders | `POST /api/v1/orders/` with an `orders:ingest` key, or store transactions when POS Receipts count store sales. | Purchase events, Last Purchase Date, Number of Purchases, a Goal's attributed revenue. |
| Coupons and loyalty tiers | C360 references synced into the workspace. | Issue Coupon (a request C360 fulfils through `coupons:issue`), Update Loyalty Tier. |
| Consent, suppressions, preferences | The preference centre, unsubscribe links, STOP replies, imports, Settings → Suppressions. | Whether a message step sends; Preview Route shows the verdict for one customer. |
| Your own systems | A Webhook step posts the customer and journey context to an https address of yours, signed. | Anything outside CEP: a CRM note, a ticket, a loyalty credit. |

## How to build a new journey

### 1. Set up: name and entry (step 1 of 2)

| Field | What to choose |
|---|---|
| Journey Name | A name unique in the workspace, for example *Welcome Series — New Loyalty Members*. |
| What Qualifies a Customer to Enter This Journey? | **Customer List**, **Event**, **Date / Time** or **Segment or Customer Change**. |
| Specific Trigger | The list, the event, the schedule, or the change (Enters Segment, Exits Segment, Loyalty Tier, Preference Update, Address Change, Customer Attribute Matches). Enters / Exits Segment also asks which **Segment**. |
| Customer Record Matches | For **Customer Attribute Matches**: the conditions a customer's record must meet to enter (see *Conditions* below), for example Customer Status is New. Everyone whose record matches enters, each once; with Every Qualifying Event, again when their record changes. A grey line says how many would enter now. |
| Only Customers Where (Optional) | Conditions that narrow any trigger: a customer the trigger would admit enters only if their record matches, for example Country is United States and Email Consent is Opted In. |
| Description (Optional) | What the journey is for; shown on its page. |

Every way a customer can enter, and what each needs:

| Entry | Enters when | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Customer List | A customer is on the chosen list (newcomers to the list enter on later passes unless the population is Fixed). | A list in Contact Lists, approved if the consent policy asks for it. |
| Event: Welcome, Birthday, Anniversary, First Purchase, Customer Creation, Purchase, Abandoned Cart, Coupon Issue, Coupon Redemption, Segment Inception, Loyalty Tier Change, Email Open/Click, SMS Response, No Response | That event arrives for the customer after the journey went live (within the last seven days at most). | The shop, app or loyalty system sending events; Attribution for purchases; nothing for Email Open/Click, SMS Response, No Response, Coupon Issue and Loyalty Tier Change, which C360 raises itself. |
| Date / Time: Daily, Weekly, Monthly, On a Specific Date | The schedule's moment comes, for the members of a list or a segment. | The list or segment. Times are the workspace's time zone; each moment fires once. |
| Date / Time: On a Date Attribute | A date on the customer's record is the chosen number of days away (every year, or once). | A custom attribute holding a date, for example `birth_date`; the Date list shows how many records have each. |
| Enters Segment, Exits Segment | The customer joins or leaves the segment. | The segment, synced from C360. |
| Loyalty Tier, Preference Update, Address Change | The attribute changes: a Loyalty Tier Change event, a preference-centre save that changed something, the Address Change event C360 sends. | The events behind them. |
| Customer Attribute Matches | The customer's record matches the conditions now. | The attributes the conditions name. |

**Date / Time entries** add their own fields under Specific Trigger, and a grey line reads the result back ("Every Monday at 9:00 AM, the members of VIP Customers enter"):

| Specific Trigger | Asks for | Who enters, and when |
|---|---|---|
| Daily | At (a time); Who Enters (a customer list or a segment) | The members, every day at that time. |
| Weekly | On (one or more weekdays); At; Who Enters | The members, on those days at that time. |
| Monthly | Day of the Month (1 to 28, or Last Day); At; Who Enters | The members, once a month. |
| On a Specific Date | Date; At; Who Enters | The members, once. |
| On a Date Attribute | Date (a date on the customer's record, with how many customers have it); When (on the day, or 1 to 90 days before or after); At; Repeat (Every Year or Once); Only Customers In (optional) | Each customer on their own date. Every Year matches the day and month (birthdays, anniversaries; 29 February counts on 28 February in other years); Once matches the full date (a renewal date). |

Choose **Next**.

### 2. Set up: population and re-entry (step 2 of 2)

| Field | What to choose |
|---|---|
| Population | **Fixed**: who qualifies on the first pass after go-live, then nobody new. **Continuously Qualifying**: new matches keep joining. **Event Triggered**: customers enter the moment the event arrives. |
| Re-Entry | **Never**: once through, never again. **After X Days**: eligible again after the number of days you enter in **Days Before Re-Entry**, counted from when they finished or exited. **Every Qualifying Event** (offered when a starter uses it): again each time the trigger happens. |

Choose **Create Journey**. The journey is created as a Draft and the builder opens.

### 3. Build the steps

The canvas shows the Entry at the top. Use the **+** between steps to add one; click a step to set it up in the panel on the right, then **Save Step**. A step still to set up is marked **Needs Setup**. The step's **⋮** menu duplicates or deletes it. **Journey Settings** on the left shows the entry, population and re-entry; **Edit Setup** there reopens the set-up. A Condition, Split or continuing Goal opens paths under it; a branching step cannot sit inside another step's path.

| Step | What it does |
|---|---|
| Wait | **For a Duration**: minutes, hours, days or weeks. **Until a Time of Day**: the customer continues at that time, in their own time zone (the `timezone` attribute on their record) or the workspace's, on any day or only on the days you pick. **Until a Date on the Record**: a date on the customer's record (a renewal date, a birthday) with an offset ("3 days before") and a time; choose whether a customer whose date has passed, or who has none, continues at once or leaves the journey (exited early, with the reason). |
| Condition | Splits into Yes and No on conditions you build (see *Conditions* below): the customer's record, this journey's messages and earlier Conditions, events, lists and segments, the goal. Optionally give the step a name, shown on its card. **Match on Entry** under the builder says how many customers in the journey would answer Yes right now. |
| Split | Several paths. **By an Attribute**: each customer takes the first path whose values hold their attribute's value (Home Store is Lakeside Mall → Path 1; Downtown or Airport → Path 2), everyone else the last path. **At Random**: percentages that add up to 100, chosen once per customer and kept on retry. Name each path; the names show on the canvas. |
| Email, SMS / MMS | Sends a published template through the normal send engine, as a campaign of its own (one per step per day), so consent, suppressions, caps and quiet hours apply. **Sender** chooses which of the workspace's senders the message goes from; left as *Workspace default*, it is the engine's default sender for the channel. An SMS step offers texting senders only. |
| Issue Coupon | Asks C360 for a coupon for the customer. C360 makes no codes: C360 collects the request, issues the coupon and marks it done (Settings → Events → Coupon Requests for C360). The step also raises a Coupon Issue event. |
| Update Loyalty Tier | Sets the customer's tier and raises a Loyalty Tier Change event. |
| Update Profile | Sets any attribute on the customer's record to a value (Customer Status to Welcomed). |
| Update Segment | Adds the customer to a segment, or removes them. |
| Add to List, Remove from List | Changes the customer's membership of a contact list. |
| Webhook | A signed JSON POST to an https address of yours with the customer (id, name, email, phone, and their custom attributes when asked), the journey, the step and the enrollment. A signing secret, kept encrypted and never shown again, puts `X-CEP-Signature: sha256=…` (HMAC-SHA256 over `timestamp.body`) beside `X-CEP-Timestamp` on every call; `X-CEP-Delivery` is unique per call. A 2xx answer is success; 429, 5xx, a timeout or a connection error is retried like any temporary failure; another 4xx is recorded and the customer carries on. **Send a Test Call** posts a sample customer. |
| Goal | The outcome the journey is for: an event (Purchase, Coupon Redemption, or any type you name), optional filters on its data (order total is at least 25), and an attribution window from entering (14 days). A customer is **converted** when the event arrives within the window: when they reach the step, or at once wherever they are in the journey. **Mark converted and exit** takes them out; **Mark converted and continue on the Reached path** opens Reached and Not Yet paths under the step (a thank-you can follow). Revenue is the matching events' amounts, as Attribution sends them. |
| Exit Journey | Ends the path. Without a Goal step, reaching an Exit counts as Goal Converted. End every path with one. |

**Save Draft** (top right) keeps your work; nothing saves on its own. **Preview Route** (top right) walks the saved draft as one customer without sending or recording anything: whether they would enter, when each wait would continue, whether each message would send under today's consent and suppressions, which way each Condition on their record answers, and where the path ends; a step that depends on something that has not happened yet asks which way to assume. When every step is set up, choose **Continue to Test Publish**.

#### Conditions

The same builder writes a Condition step, the Customer Attribute Matches trigger and the Only Customers Where filters. Each row is a field, how to compare it, and a value; **All** means every row must hold, **Any** that one is enough; **Add Group** nests one All / Any group inside the rule. A sentence under the builder reads the whole condition back. A rule holds at most 20 conditions.

| Fields | Compared with |
|---|---|
| Customer: first name, last name, email address, phone number, country, state, city, postal code, source system, record updated, last purchase date, number of purchases, and every custom attribute on the records (with its values to pick from when there are few) | Text: is, is not, is any of, is none of, contains, does not contain, starts with, is set, is not set. Numbers: equals, does not equal, is at least, is at most, is between. Dates: is within the last N days, is more than N days ago, is before, is after, is between. A custom attribute is read as text, a number or a date by the comparison you choose. |
| This Journey (Condition steps only): each Email or SMS step, any message of the journey, each other Condition, the goal | A message: was sent, was delivered, was opened, was clicked, bounced, was delivered and not opened, was not sent. A Condition: answered Yes, answered No. The goal: reached, not reached. Only sends after the customer entered count. |
| Events: the types seen in the last 90 days and the standard ones, or any type you name | Happened, did not happen; optionally within the last N days (otherwise since the customer entered the journey, or ever at entry), and filters on the event's own data (store is Lakeside, amount is at least 100). |
| Lists and segments | Is in, is not in. |

### 4. Test publish

Choose the **Test Population**: a **Random Sample of Live Audience** (up to 200 customers who would enter now) or a **Specific Test List**. Four **Readiness Checks** must pass:

| Check | Passes when |
|---|---|
| Entry rules configured | The journey has a complete entry, filters included. |
| All canvas steps configured | Every step has its settings and every path ends. |
| Templates ready | Every message step's template is published. |
| Governance rules satisfied | Always passes; it lists the frequency caps that apply ("Emails: at most 3 in 7 days") and that texts wait out quiet hours. A customer over a cap is held back, not sent. |

**Run Test Publish**, then **Send Test**. The test **sends real messages** to that population (Waits are skipped), so use the test list. The results show who got what, and how many were held back and why. Test customers leave the journey when the test ends and can still enter it for real later. Preview Route is the no-send check to use before this.

### 5. Approve and go live

**Approve & Activate →** (needs Activate). Choose **Activate Immediately** or **Schedule for Later** (a date and a time), then **Activate Journey** or **Schedule Journey**. A scheduled journey reads *Approved* until its time; **Cancel Schedule** on its page takes it back to Test.

From then on C360 enrols qualifying customers every minute. Watch them arrive on the journey's page.

## How to edit a journey

**A draft or tested journey.** **Edit Journey** on its page opens the builder on the same version. Change steps, **Save Draft**, and run Test Publish again before approving.

**The entry, population or re-entry.** In the builder, **Edit Setup** (or **Back to Setup**). On a draft everything can change. On a live journey the entry can change (as part of a new version); population and re-entry stay as they are, because they belong to the journey.

**A live journey (a new version).**
1. **Edit Journey.** The builder says the version is live. Make your changes and **Save Draft**: they are saved as the next version, and the live version keeps running untouched.
2. On the journey's page a notice says the new version is a draft; **Test Publish Version N** opens its test. Test customers already inside the live version are skipped.
3. Approve it. **Now**: new customers enter the new version at once; customers already inside finish the version they started. **Later**: the live version keeps running until that time, the page shows the date, and **Cancel Schedule** keeps the live version and leaves the new one tested, to approve again. A paused journey's new version keeps the journey paused until you resume it.

The **Versions** card lists every version with its status; the running one is tagged **Live**, a waiting one **Scheduled**.

**Goal and Exit Criteria.** On the journey's page, in Setup, the pencil beside each note edits it in place; **Save** keeps it.

**Pause, resume, end or copy.**
- **Pause Journey**: nobody new enters and nobody moves; **Resume Journey** carries on where everyone was.
- **Archive Journey**, then confirm: nobody new enters, everyone still inside leaves (counted as exited), its versions are retired and any scheduled start is dropped. It can't be undone. Archiving a live, paused or scheduled journey needs Activate.
- **Duplicate**: a new draft copy of the journey, to start again or try a variant.
- **Save as Template**: the journey kept as a template of the workspace, under Your Templates on the Journeys page (see *The ways to start a journey*). Needs Create and Edit.

## What happens behind the scenes

Four jobs run journeys. Every minute: **enroll-journey-customers** enters the customers who meet a live journey's entry (events of the last seven days at most, up to 5,000 per journey per pass; Date / Time schedules whose moment has come; attribute matches; never a customer suppressed for the journey); **advance-journey-customers** first converts anyone whose goal event arrived, then moves those whose next step is due; **activate-scheduled-journeys** takes approved journeys live when their time comes. Nightly, **rollup-journey-analytics** writes the Performance figures. One scheduler at a time works a workspace; a second one skips it.

Only customers in the customer records enter, since a message to someone unknown could never go out. Message steps send through the normal send engine, so consent, suppressions, the preference centre, frequency caps and quiet hours all apply as they do to campaigns. A step that fails for a temporary reason is tried again after 5, 15 and 60 minutes, then given up and listed under Needs Attention; a permanent failure (a missing template, a refused address) is recorded on the customer's timeline and the customer carries on.

## Things to know

- **No Response** means a customer sent at least 3 marketing emails in the last 30 days who opened, clicked and replied to none of them (security scanners left out). C360 raises it at most once in 30 days per customer, while a live journey starts from it.
- Attribute changes enter on events: **Loyalty Tier** on a Loyalty Tier Change (from the Update Loyalty Tier step, or sent by the loyalty system), **Preference Update** on a preference-centre save that changed something, **Address Change** on the event C360 sends.
- The dates for **On a Date Attribute** and **Until a Date on the Record** come from customer records' custom attributes holding a date (YYYY-MM-DD); C360 has no birthday field of its own. If no record holds a date yet, the Date list says so.
- A customer's time zone is the `timezone` custom attribute on their record, an IANA name such as America/Chicago; C360 can send it, and the upload template has a Time zone column. A record without one, or with a name C360 does not know, waits in the workspace's time zone. Test Publish skips every wait.
- A Fixed journey is closed to newcomers after its first pass, even if more customers would qualify later.
- A Condition saved before the builder (made a purchase, opened an email, and so on) keeps working as it was; opening it shows the same check as a rule, which is saved in the new form when you save the step.
- A Webhook address must be https (http only for localhost), so customer data never travels in the clear. The signing secret is kept encrypted; the Webhook Calls card never shows it.
- A saved template belongs to the workspace, not to the journey it came from: deleting the template leaves the journeys made from it as they are, and archiving the journey leaves the template. C360's own starters cannot be changed or deleted.
- The **Journeys** dataset in Reports adds up every journey's entries, conversions, revenue and messages per day over a period, with a CSV (see *Reports*).
- Events are explained in **Events** (Settings → Events), purchases in **Attribution**, keys in **API Keys**, who may do what in **Roles**.

# Events

**Where:** Settings → Integrations → Events.

## What this page is for

What happened to a customer: a purchase, an abandoned cart, a birthday, a tier change. The shop and the loyalty system send events to C360, and C360 raises some itself. A journey whose entry is an event enrols the customer within a minute, and a Condition step can check for one. This page shows what arrived, how to send events, and the coupon requests journeys leave for C360.

## Who can use it

| | |
|---|---|
| Permission | `user.admin`. The shop and the loyalty system send events with an API key that has the scope `events:ingest`; C360 reads and answers coupon requests with a key that has `coupons:issue`. The workspace needs the Journeys feature; without it events are refused. |

## The fields and controls

| Part | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Last 7 Days | How many events arrived, by type, and when the last one came. Events naming a customer C360 does not have yet are counted separately: they enter journeys once the customer arrives from C360, within seven days of the event. |
| Raised by C360 Itself | **Purchase** and **First Purchase** from every order Attribution receives (and every store sale, when Attribution counts them); **Coupon Issue** from every Issue Coupon step; **Loyalty Tier Change** from every Update Loyalty Tier step that changes the tier; **Preference Update** from every preference-centre save that changes a channel or a topic; **No Response** for lapsed engagers (below); **Email Open/Click** and **SMS Response**, read from C360's own tracking (security scanners left out). These need nothing sent. |
| Send Events | The endpoint, the headers, the key's scope and how many keys have it, and one event as an example. |
| Coupon Requests for C360 | How many requests are waiting, issued and failed, and how C360 reads and answers them. |
| Latest Events | The newest 50 events, sent or raised by CEP: when it arrived, the event and its id, the customer, the source and when it happened. Filter by type; search by customer, event id or source. Opens, clicks and text replies are not listed here. |

## The event types

Customer Creation (`CUSTOMER_CREATED`), Welcome (`WELCOME`), Birthday (`BIRTHDAY`), Anniversary (`ANNIVERSARY`), Purchase (`PURCHASE`), First Purchase (`FIRST_PURCHASE`), Category Purchase (`CATEGORY_PURCHASE`), Abandoned Cart (`CART_ABANDON`), Coupon Issue (`COUPON_ISSUED`), Coupon Redemption (`COUPON_REDEEMED`), Segment Inception (`SEGMENT_INCEPTION`), Segment Change (`SEGMENT_CHANGE`), Loyalty Tier Change (`LOYALTY_TIER_CHANGE`), Preference Update (`PREFERENCE_UPDATE`), Address Change (`ADDRESS_CHANGE`), No Response (`NO_RESPONSE`), Email Open/Click (`EMAIL_ENGAGEMENT`) and SMS Response (`SMS_RESPONSE`). Any other name in capitals and underscores (for example `STORE_VISIT`) is accepted too.

A journey's **Attribute Change** entry reads these: Loyalty Tier on `LOYALTY_TIER_CHANGE`, Preference Update on `PREFERENCE_UPDATE`, Address Change on `ADDRESS_CHANGE`. C360 does not keep a history of customer records, so the loyalty system sends the tier changes it makes, and C360 sends `ADDRESS_CHANGE` when an address changes (the new address in `payload`).

**No Response** is raised by C360 for a customer sent at least 3 marketing emails (not receipts) in the last 30 days who opened, clicked and replied to none of them, security scanners left out. One per customer in 30 days, and only while a live journey starts from it (checked every 15 minutes).

## How to

**Connect the shop or the loyalty system.**
1. Settings → API Keys → New Key, with the scope `events:ingest`. Copy the key; it is shown once.
2. Give the developer the endpoint, the key and the example from Send Events.
3. Each event needs `event_type` and the customer by `party_id`, `email` or `phone` (an email or number must match a customer). Add `event_id` so an event sent twice counts once; `occurred_at` defaults to now; `payload` holds the details (for example the cart's value).
4. The first events appear in Latest Events within a minute.

**Let C360 fulfil coupons.**
1. A key with the scope `coupons:issue`.
2. C360 calls `GET /api/v1/coupon-issuances/` for the waiting requests (the customer, the coupon, the journey).
3. For each, it issues the coupon and calls `PATCH /api/v1/coupon-issuances/<id>/` with `{"status": "ISSUED", "external_code": "…"}`, or `"FAILED"` with a `note`.

**Check that an abandoned-cart journey will fire.** Latest Events → Type: Abandoned Cart. If nothing is there, the shop is not sending; if the customer reads *Not in the customer records yet*, the customer has not arrived from C360.

## What happens behind the scenes

Events arrive at `POST /api/v1/events/`, one at a time or up to 500 as `{"events": [...]}`. Each comes back Accepted, Duplicate (the same `event_id` from the same source was already received) or Rejected with the reason (an unknown type format, no customer, an email or number that matches nobody, a time more than a day ahead or more than 400 days back). A call where every event is rejected answers 400. Every call is in Settings → Integrations' call log.

The enrol job reads events every minute. An event counts for a journey that was live when it happened, at most seven days back.

## Things to know

- C360 keeps the event's type, customer, time, source, id and payload (up to 8,000 characters), not more.
- C360 never makes coupon codes; C360 does.

# Insights: Reports, Dashboards and Scheduled Delivery

**Where:** Insights, in the app launcher (the grid icon at the top left), and the Insights item in Engagement's menu, which opens the same reports.

## What Insights is for

Insights is the suite's reporting app. It lets you build your own reports on the data C360 already keeps, your messages, the orders they led to, your contacts and your journeys, see them as a grid, a chart, a hierarchy or a pivot, put them on a dashboard with one period for all of them, export them as CSV, Excel or PDF, and have them emailed on a schedule. Nothing is collected for it; it reads what the rest of the suite records.

## Who can use it

| Permission | What it allows |
|---|---|
| Reports · View (`report.view`) | Open and run the reports and dashboards shared with the workspace, export them, star favourites, switch a report's view. |
| Reports · Edit (`report.edit`) | Make and change your own reports, dashboards, folders and schedules; delete your own. |
| Report Admin (`report.admin`) | Everything on every report, dashboard, folder and schedule in the workspace; build on personal-data fields; see the Run Log. |
| User Admin (`user.admin`) | Also builds on personal-data fields. |

The workspace needs the Insights component switched on by the platform team; without it the menu does not offer Insights.

## The datasets

A report is built on one dataset. Each offers **dimensions** (things to group by, such as Campaign, Channel or Sent month) and **measures** (numbers to total, such as Sent Count or Revenue). The datasets today:

| Dataset | One row per | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Messages | Every email and text a campaign sent | Sends, deliveries, opens and clicks by campaign, channel or month. |
| Attributed Orders | Every order the shop reported and the campaign it was credited to | Revenue by campaign, channel, store or month. |
| Contacts | Every contact in the workspace | Contacts by city, region, source or consent. |
| Journey Enrolments | Every customer in a journey | Enrolments, completions and conversions by journey. |

Fields marked **PII** carry personal data (a customer's identifier, an email address). Building on them needs Report Admin or User Admin. When you open a shared report that has such a column and you lack the right, its values are masked and the grid says so.

## Reports

### The list

Insights → Reports lists every report you can open, grouped by the component whose data it uses, with its view, dataset, owner and last run. Your **favourites** sit first: press the star on any row to add one. **Folders** group a workspace's reports; the chips at the top filter by folder or by No Folder, and the folder shows under the report's name. Search finds a report by name.

### Making a report

1. Press **New Report**, pick a dataset and give it a name. It opens in the editor with the dataset's first columns.
2. Build it with one of the two editors, switched at the top right and remembered for you:
   - **Field Wells**: the dataset's fields on the left, the result in the middle, the wells on the right. Click or drag a field into **Rows** (one line per value), **Columns** (one dimension spread across, for the pivot and the chart's series) or **Values** (a number to total, with its calculation). **Filters**, then **Sort and Limit**, sit under them.
   - **Steps**: the question written top to bottom as numbered steps: Data, Filter, Summarize, Group By, Sort and Limit. Each step has an Add button; the result sits under the steps.
   A date dimension can be grouped **by Day, Week, Month, Quarter or Year**. A measure has its calculation: Sum, Count, Distinct Count, Average, Min or Max.
3. **Filters**: one row per rule (field, operator, value), joined with **and** or **or**. A list is comma-separated, a range is from..to, "in the last days" takes a number. Dates are the workspace's time zone.
4. The preview runs as you build, up to the preview cap. The line above it says how many rows, how long, and how fresh the data is.
5. **Save** writes a new version. Cancel drops the changes.

### The four views

Any report switches between the views without losing its fields; the switch sits above the result, in the editor and in the viewer. The editor saves the choice; the viewer switches for a look.

| View | What it shows | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| Grid | One row per record or group, sortable and resizable columns | Anything |
| Chart | Bars, lines or area: the first dimension along the bottom, a series per value of the dimension across (else one per measure), the legend to show and hide, Show the Numbers under it | A dimension and a measure |
| Hierarchy | The dimensions in their order, each group folding over the next, a subtotal per group, Expand All and Collapse All | Two dimensions and a measure |
| Pivot | The dimension across as columns, one measure in the cells (a select when there are several), a total on both edges | A dimension down, one across, a measure |

Subtotals and totals come from the engine, so averages and distinct counts are exact, and a report longer than the page still totals everything.

### The viewer

Opening a report runs it and shows the saved view. Above the rows: the view switch; under the title: what the run says (rows, time, freshness, version) and, once scheduled, the schedule line. The buttons:

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| Folder | Files the report in a folder, or in a new one (owner or administrator). |
| Versions | Every saved version with who, when and the note. **Open** shows one as it was, read-only, with Back to Current. **Restore** makes it the current version again, as a new version; nothing is lost. |
| Schedule | Emails the report on a schedule (below). |
| Edit | The editor (owner or administrator). |
| Export CSV, Export Excel, Export PDF | The report as a file: CSV the rows; Excel the view with its totals first and the rows after, with number formats; PDF the view styled for paper. |
| Delete | Removes the report and its versions (owner or administrator). |

## Dashboards

A dashboard is a page of tiles, each tile a saved report in one of its views or one of its figures, on **one period** that applies to every tile.

- **Insights → Dashboards** lists them, favourites first, with their tiles, owner and sharing.
- **New Dashboard** opens an empty one in the editor. Name it, choose who can open it (only you, or the workspace), and **Add Tile**: pick a report, then how to show it, one of its views or **one figure**, a measure totalled over the period. Each new tile goes into the last row with room, else a new row.
- A dashboard is **rows of tiles**: one to four across of equal width, or wide and narrow. The row bar sets the width and moves or removes the row; a tile's controls move it left or right, rename it or remove it. Nothing overlaps or leaves a gap. **Done** saves the layout as a version.
- **The period**: 7 Days, 30 Days, This Month, Quarter, or Custom dates, in the workspace's time zone, applied to every tile through its dataset's date. A report with its own date filter keeps it and the tile says **Own dates**. **Compare With Previous Period** adds the change to every figure.
- Viewing a dashboard, you can switch the period and the comparison for a look without saving.
- **Export PDF** gives the dashboard with its tiles on its saved period; **Schedule** emails it as PDF.

## Scheduled delivery

Press **Schedule** on a report or a dashboard:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Every | Day, Weekday, Week (on a day) or Month (on a day of the month, 1 to 28). |
| At | The time, in the workspace's time zone. |
| Send as | Excel, CSV or PDF for a report; a dashboard goes as PDF. |
| To | Up to twenty addresses; Enter, a comma or a space adds one. |
| Subject | Optional. `{date}` becomes the sending date. Empty: the report's name and the date. |
| Note | Optional, at the top of the email. |

The email goes out through the workspace's own mail server (Settings → Email Server; a workspace without one cannot schedule), built at the moment of sending with the rights and masking of whoever scheduled it, the file attached. Under the title the schedule line shows what, when, to how many, the last sending and the next, with **Change** and **Pause**. **Send a Test Now**, on a saved schedule, sends the email to you at once. **Insights → Scheduled** lists every schedule you may see, with Pause, Resume and Delete. Three failures in a row pause a schedule; Resume starts it again.

## Run Log

Insights → Run Log, for report administrators: every preview, run, export and delivery of the last 7 or 30 days, with the report and its version, the kind, who, the outcome with its reason when it is not OK, the rows and the time, filterable by outcome, person and report name, with a summary of the period.

## How fresh is the data

The line under a report's title says it. **Live data**: the report reads the workspace's database as it is. **Data as of the last quarter hour**: the workspace reports on the analytics engine, which the platform team loads from the database every fifteen minutes; new sends show on the next load. The platform team decides which engine a workspace is on; the pages and the figures are the same either way.

## When something does not run

| Message | What it means |
|---|---|
| The report took longer than 15 seconds and was stopped | Narrow it with a filter or a shorter period. |
| X is personal data: building a report on it needs the report administration right | Ask an administrator to build that part, or to grant the right. |
| This workspace's reports are moving to a new engine | The platform team is switching the engine; try again shortly. |
| This workspace has no mail server yet | Set one up under Settings → Email Server before scheduling. |
| Insights is not part of this workspace | Ask the platform team to switch the component on. |
