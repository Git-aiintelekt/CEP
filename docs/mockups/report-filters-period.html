Mockup, not the product. Static HTML on the app's colour and shape values. Sample filters from the Citi SMS reports.

What changes. Every report on data with a date asks for a Period above its rows: 7 Days, 15 Days, 30 Days, Quarter, Half Year, Year or a Custom range. It defaults to 7 Days and can't be removed, so no report reads a whole table unless someone asks for it. Authors add more filters people choose (Campaign, Store, Channel…) in the report editor. Periods are rolling: Quarter is the last 3 months, Half Year the last 6, Year the last 12.

What gets built, in words

  • Period on every report with a date, on the dataset's time field: 7 Days, 15 Days, 30 Days, Quarter (3 months), Half Year (6), Year (12), Custom. Default 7 Days, always asked; the author may change the default. New reports, AI drafts and saves get it; a command gives it to the reports made before. Reports on data without a date (a store list) don't ask it.
  • Campaign reports ask Campaign and Period; Period replaces the Send Date question added earlier today, and the latest campaign is the latest within the period (none sent in 7 days: every campaign of the period).
  • Dashboards keep their own period: on a tile the dashboard's period takes the place of the report's. Schedules and alerts use the report's default, 7 days.
  • Report Filters in the editor: the Parameters dialog, renamed and moved to the top bar with a count, up to eight filters, Period locked at the top.
  • Speed: 7 days of SMS receipts is about 250 thousand rows instead of 1.1 million for 30 days, and the period reads the date-and-time column, which the Citi index can use.
Recommendation: Option A. The period is the filter people change most; one row of chips shows every choice and takes one click, and Custom stays beside it. Option B is tidier when a report has many filters, but hides the choices behind a click.
