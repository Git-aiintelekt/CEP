Tenant onboarding. Today a tenant is one admin form (Tenants → Add) that runs the provisioning on save, and the rest (plan, first administrator, policies) lives on other admin pages with the checklist spreadsheet as the guide. The pictures below put the whole set-up into one guided flow in the back-office, and give every tenant a readiness panel that shows where it stands. The workspace's own set-up (engines, senders, lists) stays with its administrator in Settings: the back-office hands it off, it does not do it.

Option 1 — a guided flow, Tenants → Onboard a Tenant, six stepsEvery value the platform owns is asked once, in order, with what it means next to it. The last step runs the provisioning and shows what it did. Busy: a Retail workspace on Growth with everything filled in; sparse: the minimum (General business on Starter, defaults everywhere).

What gets built, in words

  • The flow (Option 1): a custom admin view at /admin/tenants/tenant/onboard/ with the six steps kept in the session until Review; it writes tenant, tenant_subscription (TRIAL with trial_ends_at), the first app_user with its user_role, the workspace's settings (industry, feature overrides, consent_policy, quiet hours when changed) and then runs services.onboard_tenant and the password-link mail, in one transaction except the mail.
  • Readiness (Option 2, and the landing of Option 1): eight checks computed from existing tables (partitions, routing row, roles, subscription, settings, users with user.admin, platform tags present, identity provider); a panel on the tenant's change page and a column on the list. "Workspace sending" reads the workspace's engines and senders read-only.
  • Hand-off: the password-link mail template gains the set-up list and the optional line; Draft and Not onboarded appear as statuses the list can filter.
  • Unchanged: Tenants → Add keeps working for a bare row; Repair onboarding stays; dedicated hosting stays a tenant operation after the flow.
